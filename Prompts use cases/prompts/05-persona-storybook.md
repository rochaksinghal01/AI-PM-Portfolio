# Persona Storybook — Hero Narrative

## What This Prompt Does
Generates a cinematic, emotionally resonant 250–400 word user story that puts your product feature as the "hero" in a real person's day. The narrative arc moves from daily struggle → friction → tension → product saves the day → transformation → new routine. Culturally grounded, bias-free, and ready to use in decks, onboarding flows, or stakeholder presentations.

## When to Use
- Product strategy decks — bring data to life with a human story
- Feature launch internal comms or all-hands
- Onboarding flows that need empathy, not just instructions
- Persona presentations to make abstract user segments feel real
- Sales demos — open with a story, not a feature list

## Inputs Required
1. Product or feature to highlight
2. Persona's name
3. Region or city the story is set in

---

## Prompt

```
You are a story creator crafting a cinematic, empathetic persona story.

Ask me only these 3 inputs in one message:
1. What is the product/feature to highlight?
2. What is the target persona's name?
3. Which region/city is this story set in?

After I answer, do not ask anything else. Produce the story immediately.

Story requirements (apply automatically):

- Length: 250–400 words, 6–8 short paragraphs
- Title + 1-line logline on top

Narrative arc:
Before → daily struggle with sensory detail and stakes
Inciting friction → something goes wrong or pressure mounts
Rising tension → time pressure, context, real constraints
Product feature enters as the "hero" → name the feature, show how it works concretely
Transformation → specific outcomes: relief, control, time saved
After → new routine, confidence, meaningful gain
Close → hopeful image + soft call-to-action

Tone rules:
- Empathetic, vivid, human
- Plain language — no jargon, no buzzwords
- Culturally respectful to the given region (use natural names, local moments, authentic cues)

Include:
- 1 short line of natural dialogue
- 1–2 italicised inner thoughts from the persona (keep brief and tasteful)
- 3 concrete product mechanisms woven into sentences (e.g., "auto-sorts receipts," "predicts peak traffic," "schedules recurring orders") — never as bullet lists

Constraints:
- No hype or unrealistic claims
- No medical or financial promises
- Privacy-safe and brand-safe
- Emotion arc: frustration / overwhelm / anxiety → calm / time saved / confidence gained
- End with a subtle CTA that fits the story naturally (e.g., "Tonight, she taps 'Start'.")

Output format (strict):
Title
Logline (one sentence)
Story (paragraphs only — no bullets, no meta commentary, no section headers)

Internal generation steps (do not display):
1. Build a brief outline
2. Map the persona's day
3. Select 2–3 region-authentic details
4. Integrate the feature's mechanisms where they naturally resolve pain
5. Convert to prose
6. Polish for rhythm and clarity

Begin by asking only:
"Product/feature?"
"Persona name?"
"Region/city?"
Then generate the story.
```

---

## Example Output Structure

```
[Title]
[One-line logline]

[Para 1 — Before: Priya's Tuesday morning in Mumbai. The 6:47 train...]
[Para 2 — Inciting friction: But today the brief landed at midnight...]
[Para 3 — Rising tension: Three clients. One hour. No plan.]
[Para 4 — Feature enters: She opened the app and typed...]
[Para 5 — Transformation: In four minutes, what used to take a day...]
[Para 6 — After: That evening, she closed her laptop at 6.]
[Para 7 — Close + CTA: Tonight, she taps 'Start'.]
```

---

## Tips
- The more specific the region, the better the cultural grounding. "Mumbai" beats "India"; "Koramangala, Bangalore" beats "Bangalore".
- If you want the story for a specific channel (deck slide, email, onboarding tooltip), append: "Keep it suitable for [channel]."
- Run two versions — one for a power user and one for a first-time user — and compare which resonates in user testing.
